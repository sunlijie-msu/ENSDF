2) Rounding and Uncertainty Convention:

Successive (Sequential) Rounding.
We employ a Successive Rounding methodology where values are processed digit-by-digit from the rightmost decimal place moving leftward.

Rounding Threshold: Round Half-Up (5-Up)
For general calculated values, we apply the Standard Round Half-Up convention.
Down: Digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 are truncated (the preceding digit remains unchanged).
Up: Digits 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 cause the preceding digit to be incremented by 1.

Application example:
0.344 → 0.34 → 0.3, the last 4 rounds down; then the 4 rounds down.
0.345 → 0.35 → 0.4, the 5 rounds the 4 up; then the new 5 rounds the 3 up.

Uncertainty and Significant Digits
The final reported value is rounded to match the decimal place of the least significant digit in the uncertainty. The uncertainty digits are rounded according to the threshold (4-Up)

Application example:
100.00(333) → 100.0(33), last '3' rounds down; uncertainty stays '33'
100.00(334) → 100.0(34), last '4' rounds the '3' up; uncertainty becomes '34'



3) Averaging Convention:
We use the default option in the Java Averaging tool across all datasets.

Choice on weighted vs unweighted average:
For example, in the 35S Adopted Dataset, there is an Iγ from the weighted average of 73 9 from (14N,3pγ) and 36 18 from (18O,2αnγ)

------ average X------
Data points of X record
*                      (14N,3p)   73(9)               weight=80.00%
*                     (18O,2n)   36(18)              weight=20.00%

Averaging results:
           weighted average:      66(8)                (internal)
                                  66(15)               (external)
                                  chi**2/(n-1)=3.380     [critical=4.605]
         unweighted average:      55(19)              
           (of all values)        chi**2/(n-1)=684.500     [critical=4.605]

   suggested adopted result:      66(15)              
       (Weighted-Of-All)

So, we choose the weighted average as the adopted value.

Minimum Uncertainty Rule:
The tool implements the Minimum Uncertainty Rule: the final adopted uncertainty must be greater than or equal to any individual input uncertainty. This rule ensures that averaging never artificially reduces systematic uncertainty below the best single measurement, and maintains conservative uncertainty estimates in nuclear data evaluation.




5) Incomplete Decay Scheme:
For an incomplete decay scheme, we usually have comments like:
I|b{+-} is from the |g+ce intensity balance at each state. Quoted I|b{+-} values without uncertainties are considered upper limits due to the incomplete decay scheme.
Reviewer: "Then why not put "LE" in the uncertainty column?"

Quoted Iβ- values without uncertainties are considered upper limits due to the incomplete decay scheme by the evaluators, even though they are not reported as limits in the source literature. Because the decay scheme is considered incomplete, it is not justified to adopt uncertainties from normalization using only observed gamma transitions, as discussed in past USNDP meetings. By contrast, an explicit "LE" in the uncertainty column would indicate that an upper limit is explicitly reported in the literature or that the evaluators have adopted/recommended that limit---either of which is not the case here and using it would be misleading.
