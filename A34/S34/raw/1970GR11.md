### **Experimental Study of \\(^{34}\text{S}\\) (Greene et al., 1970)**

A high-signal summary of the experimental parameters, measurements, and physical deductions from the 1970Gr11 paper is presented below:

---

#### **1. Facility and Beams**
* **Facility:** University of **Liverpool EN tandem accelerator**.
* **Beam:** \\(^4\text{He}^{++}\\) ions (alpha particles).
* **Energy:** Bombarding energies ranged from **\\(6.0\text{ to } 9.0\text{ MeV}\\)** for excitation and decay scheme determinations. Detailed Doppler shift attenuation measurements were performed at a fixed energy of **\\(7.00\text{ MeV}\\)**.

#### **2. Target**
* **Material:** A target of natural, red **\\(^{31}\text{P}\\)**.
* **Backing:** Deposited on a gold (**\\(\text{Au}\\)**) backing.
* **Thickness:** **\\(950 \pm 30\ \mu\text{g/cm}^2\\)**, thick enough to fully stop the recoiling \\(^{34}\text{S}\\) nuclei within the target medium.

#### **3. Radiation, Particles, and Detectors**
* **Radiation Detected:** **Gamma-rays (\\(\gamma\\)-rays)**. No reaction particles (such as protons) were detected in coincidence.
* **Spectrometer:** A specialized **\\(\text{Ge(Li)}-\text{NaI(Tl)}\\) anti-Compton and pair escape spectrometer** consisting of:
  * **Primary Detector:** A **\\(40\text{ cm}^3\ \text{Ge(Li)}\\)** coaxial detector positioned **\\(25\text{ cm}\\)** from the target.
  * **Shielding:** A **\\(10\text{ cm}\\)** thick lead shield with a conical collimator subtending **\\(\pm 4^\circ\\)** to prevent target-scattered radiation from directly hitting the annulus.
  * **Guard Detector:** A **\\(20\text{ cm}\\)** long **\\(\text{NaI(Tl)}\\)** scintillation annulus split into quadrants surrounding the \\(\text{Ge(Li)}\\) detector.
* **Detection Modes:**
  * **Anti-Compton Mode:** Anti-coincidences between the \\(\text{Ge(Li)}\\) and the \\(\text{NaI(Tl)}\\) quadrants were recorded to suppress the Compton scattering background.
  * **Pair Escape Mode:** Triple coincidences between the \\(\text{Ge(Li)}\\) and two opposite quadrants of the annulus were recorded to select clean double-escape peaks.

#### **4. Measured Data**
* **Energy-Dependence Spectra:** Both anti-Compton and pair escape spectra were measured at **\\(90^\circ\\)** relative to the beam direction at \\(E_\alpha = 6.0, 7.0, 8.0,\\) and \\(9.0\text{ MeV}\\) to identify excitation thresholds and decay modes.
* **Doppler Shifts:** Gamma-ray peak centroids were measured at **\\(7.00\text{ MeV}\\)** across **six angles** between \\(0^\circ\\) and \\(130^\circ\\) (\\(90^\circ\\) data was discarded due to electronic gain instability).
* **Branching Yields:** Spectra measured at **\\(50^\circ\\) and \\(130^\circ\\)** were analyzed to extract relative transition intensities.
* **Calibration Data:** Spectra of a **\\(^{56}\text{Co}\\)** standard source were acquired before and after measurement runs to monitor and correct for electronic zero-drift.

#### **5. Deduced Information & Analysis Methods**

##### **Deduced Information:**
* **Doublet Resolution:** Resolved a previously ambiguous energy level near \\(4.88\text{ MeV}\\) into a close doublet of states at **\\(4874 \pm 2\text{ keV}\\)** and **\\(4892 \pm 3\text{ keV}\\)**.
* **Excitation Energies and Decay Schemes:** Accurate determinations of branching percentages and level excitation energies for states up to \\(5.3\text{ MeV}\\).
* **Mean Lifetimes (\\(\tau_m\\)):** Precise lifetimes or upper limits for **10 excited states** below \\(5.5\text{ MeV}\\) (summarized in the table below).
* **Transition Strengths:** Electromagnetic transition strengths in **Weisskopf units (W.u.)** for M1, E1, and E2 modes.
* **Structure Interpretation:** Discussed nuclear wavefunctions within a **weak coupling framework**, classifying states as collective-vibrational (resembling a vibrating \\(^{32}\text{S}\\) core) or single-particle in nature.

##### **Analysis Methods:**
* **Doppler Shift Attenuation Method (DSAM):** Experimental attenuation factors (\\(F\\)) were extracted by a least-squares fit of peak centroids as a function of the detection angle (\\(\theta\\)) to the relation:
  \\[E_\gamma = E_0(1 + F\beta \cos\theta)\\]
* **Stopping Power Theory:** Lifetimes (\\(\tau_m\\)) were calculated by comparing experimental \\(F\\)-values with theoretical calculations based on **Lindhard, Scharff, and Schiøtt (LSS) stopping power theory**, incorporating **Blaugrund’s formalism** to correct for nuclear scattering.
* **Cascade Feeding Corrections:** Applied correction calculations for higher-state cascade feeding (e.g., \\(12\%\\) correction for the \\(2127\text{ keV}\\) level).
* **Spectrometer Efficiency Fitting:** Extrapolated efficiency curves derived from the \\(^{56}\text{Co}\\) source standard to calculate branching ratios from \\(\gamma\\)-ray photopeak and escape-peak intensities.
